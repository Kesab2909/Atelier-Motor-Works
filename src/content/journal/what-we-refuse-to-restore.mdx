We turn away more commissions than we accept. This is not out of arrogance, but out of duty. A restoration requires a foundation of truth. If a chassis is irreparably compromised, or if a client desires a soulless restomod that destroys the original character, we respectfully decline. Our responsibility is first to the machine, and second to the client.

### The Foundation of Truth

Every vintage vehicle that enters our facility is subjected to a rigorous inspection process. We are looking for something very specific: honesty. A car that has been crashed, poorly repaired, rusted through the structural bulkheads, and welded back together by a well-meaning amateur in the 1980s is often a lost cause. While any amount of metal can technically be replaced, there comes a point where the original vehicle ceases to exist, and you are simply building a replica around a VIN tag.

We refuse to participate in the erasure of a vehicle's soul. If a car requires 80% of its tub to be fabricated from scratch, it is no longer the machine that left Stuttgart or Munich. It is a new car wearing an old name. For us, a true restoration must preserve the vast majority of the original factory steel. We are willing to spend hundreds of hours repairing original metal, but we draw the line at wholesale replacement of the structural core.

### The Problem with Restomods

The current trend of the "restomod"—taking a vintage shell and stuffing it full of modern crate engines, digital dashboards, and enormous brakes—is incredibly popular. But it is not what we do. 

When a client approaches us asking to put an electric drivetrain into a classic 911, or to fit a digital iPad-style screen into the dashboard of an E30 M3, we politely decline. The magic of a vintage car is exactly that: it is vintage. It is analog. It requires skill, patience, and compromise to drive. If you want the reliability and seamless power delivery of a modern sports car, the market is full of excellent options. But do not destroy a piece of history to achieve it. 

Our upgrades are always period-correct or sympathetic to the era. We will improve suspension geometry using components that look factory. We will build an engine to its maximum naturally aspirated potential using authentic Weber carburetors or mechanical injection. But we will not digitize an analog experience.

### Custodianship over Ownership

Ultimately, our refusal to take on certain projects stems from our belief in custodianship. We believe that nobody truly "owns" a significant piece of automotive history. You are merely taking care of it for the next generation. If a proposed project permanently alters the historical accuracy of a significant chassis, we will not perform the work. We owe it to the original engineers, and to the future enthusiasts who will inherit these cars, to keep them as pure as possible. 

By saying no to the wrong projects, we ensure that every hour of our capacity is dedicated to the right ones.
